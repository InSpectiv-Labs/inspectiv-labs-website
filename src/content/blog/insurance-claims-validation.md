# Automating Subsidence Claims Validation

*This is placeholder content. To update this post, edit `/src/content/blog/insurance-claims-validation.md`.*

When a major geological event occurs—such as severe drought leading to clay shrinkage, or localized seismic activity—property insurers are flooded with subsidence claims. Traditionally, verifying these claims requires dispatching structural engineers to hundreds of properties, a process that is agonizingly slow, incredibly expensive, and prone to subjective interpretation.

## The Problem with Manual Adjudication

Determining whether a crack in a foundation is a result of a recent, covered event or pre-existing, long-term settlement is the core challenge of subsidence claims. Without historical context, adjusters often err on the side of the claimant to avoid disputes, leading to massive fraud exposure and inflated payouts.

## The Satellite Advantage

By utilizing InSAR (Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar), insurers can dramatically overhaul their claims processing pipeline. 

### 1. The Historical "Time Machine"
Satellites have been recording millimeter-scale ground movement for years. When a claim is filed, insurers can look back at the precise movement history of that specific property over the last three years. Did the foundation suddenly drop last month, or has it been sinking steadily since 2021?

### 2. Wide-Area Triage
Following a regional event, InSAR data allows insurers to instantly generate a risk map of the affected area. Claims originating from the "red zones" (areas of verified, severe movement) can be fast-tracked and paid out automatically, drastically reducing turnaround time and improving customer satisfaction.

### 3. Defensible Denials
Conversely, claims originating from stable "green zones," or claims where historical data proves pre-existing damage, can be confidently denied. The satellite data provides a highly defensible, objective piece of evidence that drastically reduces litigation risk.
